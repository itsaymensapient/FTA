Why Cloudera is pivotal for senior customers
Senior data and technology leaders face the same three questions on AI: can we use our most sensitive data, can
we control the cost, and can we prove it is governed? Cloudera's relevance comes from where it already sits in the
estate.
 It already holds the data. Cloudera says its platform spans about 30 exabytes of data. AI can start from data the
customer already owns and governs, with no migration first.
 It is built for regulated industries. Financial services, healthcare, telecommunications, manufacturing and the
public sector are the named targets for its secure AI deployment.
 Sovereignty and private AI. Models, prompts and data can stay behind the customer's firewall, including
air-gapped environments.
 Predictable cost. On-premises inference is positioned with fixed pricing and a lower total cost than token-based
cloud APIs.
 One governance layer. Shared Data Experience (SDX) applies the same security and compliance rules to data,
models and agents, wherever they run.
 Open standards, less lock-in. Apache Iceberg and the Polaris catalog let other engines read the same data
without copying it.
 Analyst validation. Cloudera is named a leader in The Forrester Wave: Data Fabric Platforms, Q4 2025.
The executive message in one sentence: AI projects stall when sensitive data cannot leave the building;
Cloudera lets the AI come to the data, with the governance the board expects.

Latest innovations
Cloudera Anywhere Cloud
Announced on 19 August 2026 at EVOLVE in Singapore. It is a new platform for building, deploying and scaling
production data and AI applications across multi-cloud, on-premises and sovereign environments. It can run
standalone or alongside a customer's existing Cloudera data lake.
 Agentic copilot: turns plain-language requests into automated data workflows or infrastructure changes.
 Self-service marketplace: blueprints launch AI applications and analytics pipelines in minutes, with security
inherited automatically.
 Open foundation: Apache Iceberg, Polaris catalog and unified APIs; zero-copy queries on data in place.
 Layered architecture: foundation services, a Unified Data Fabric (policy, governance, lineage, audit) and
independently deployable Data Services 2.x built on Spark, Flink, Kafka, NiFi, Trino and Airflow.
 Governed agents: sandboxed execution, audit trails and human approval gates for sensitive actions.
 Hadoop-free: new AI and analytics workloads go to Anywhere Cloud; existing customers get six years of support
on the current platform, and older data services can coexist with the new ones.
 Design partners: ADMIRAL Technologies, ExxonMobil, IQVIA, IXEN.ai and Mastercard, among others. During
evaluation ExxonMobil reported deployment time falling from about six hours to about 65 minutes.
 Status: technical preview today; general availability is scheduled for 30 November 2026.
Cloudera Blueprints
Announced at EVOLVE26 New York in late October 2026. Blueprints are ready-to-deploy AI packages that pair
NVIDIA's reference workflows with Cloudera's data management, security and governance. The first four cover
financial transaction analysis, automated clinical documentation, telecommunications network configuration and
synthetic video detection.
Private AI in the data centre
In early 2026 Cloudera extended AI Inference and Data Warehouse with Trino to on-premises environments, so
customers can serve models and run federated analytics behind their own firewall.
Acquired technology now in the platform
Taikun (Kubernetes-based orchestration), Verta (model management and low-code tools) and Octopai (data lineage
and cataloguing) contribute to the new architecture.

Cloudera AI Workbench: where teams build
A secure, shared workspace where data scientists and developers build, train, fine-tune and deploy models on
enterprise data, across hybrid, multi-cloud or on-premises environments.
 Familiar tools: Jupyter, VS Code or RStudio, in a browser or on a laptop.
 Custom runtimes: isolated, containerised sessions for Python, R, Scala, Spark and GPU work.
 Scale-out compute: resources scale up and down to match the workload.
 AMPs (Applied ML Prototypes): ready-to-run project templates that give teams a working starting point.
 Built-in governance: security and compliance handled through SDX.
 Deploy apps and agents: host them while data, models and infrastructure stay under customer control.
Cloudera AI Studios: build GenAI with less code
A suite of low-code tools, opened from inside AI Workbench. Teams can start in a visual interface and move to full
code when they need more control. All four studios work together within one project.

Cloudera AI Inference: where models run in production
Inference is the moment a model does its job: answering a question or scoring a transaction. AI Inference is the
production-grade service that hosts both predictive models and generative AI, and keeps them fast, secure and
monitored.
 Powered by NVIDIA: tight integration with NIM microservices and Triton Inference Server.
 Performance (Cloudera's claim): up to 36x faster inference on NVIDIA GPUs and nearly 4x throughput on
CPUs.
 Scale: 100 or more model endpoints at once, given correctly sized compute; autoscaling and high availability.
 Any model type: traditional ML, LLMs and other GenAI; TensorFlow, PyTorch, Scikit-learn, Hugging Face.
 Standard APIs: OpenAI-compatible API for LLMs and Open Inference Protocol for traditional models, so existing
applications connect easily.
 Real-time and batch: low-latency answers and large batch jobs.
 Registry and lineage: models are stored and versioned in Cloudera AI Registry, with data and model lineage for
audit.
 Monitoring: latency, throughput and model health, plus GenAI measures such as sentiment, feedback and drift.
 Security: authentication, and encryption in motion and at rest.
Option

Cloudera on cloud
Multi-cloud deployment, quick start with no infrastructure set-up, and scale-to-zero autoscaling
so idle GPUs do not cost money.
Cloudera on premises

with fixed pricing.

 "Coming soon" items: the AI Inference page still marks canary, blue-green and A/B rollouts, authorization and
open management APIs as coming soon.

evaluation, and the analyst notes results will vary by workload.
