GITEX Dubai: CDP with AI agents
Stand scenario: inspector and business
1. Stand concept
The stand shows the CDP working with AI agents. A visitor types a name and chooses a role: FTA Inspector or Business. Both roles work on the same synthetic data: e-invoices, VAT returns, refund requests and FTA letters.
The stand has four scenarios and a free query. Each scenario is solved from both sides. The inspector, working with the FTA agent, finds the problem and writes to the company. The taxpayer, working with the FTA Assistant, works out what went wrong and replies. Dates, amounts and letter numbers are the same in both roles, so a visitor who plays both sees one story from two sides. Scenario 1 runs live: in the Business role the visitor issues an e-invoice from a seller's e-invoicing app and sees it in the FTA system with the check results; in the Inspector role the same invoice appears as a fresh event. The stand is one station; the visitor chooses the role.
The dates in the scenarios are real calendar dates and depend on the date of the event: "today" on the stand is a day of the event, the FTA letters are dated that day, and the replies come a few days later. In this document the dates are set for GITEX GLOBAL 2026 (8–11 December 2026): the inspector's "today" is Thursday 10.12.2026, the business "today" is Friday 11.12.2026. If the event moves, all dates move with it. Scenario 1 uses the real date and time of the stand.

The six steps on both sides
Every scenario goes through the same six steps on both sides. A step tracker stays at the top of the screen.

The six steps: FTA Inspector and Business
Step
Inspector with the FTA agent
Business with the FTA Assistant
1 Signal / Check
The briefing lists a fresh flagged e-invoice and three events ordered by risk. Each card shows a colour, the reason (for example "High · refund decision due 25 Dec"), the company and one line on the issue. The agent greets the visitor by name and says what it has checked since the last session. The visitor opens an event or types a question.
"Choose a company" shows four companies: three with an open issue and its deadline, and Najmat Al Khaleej, where the visitor issues an e-invoice live. There are no risk ratings on this side. On the company overview the assistant greets the visitor and shows the issue card, the FTA letters with their deadlines and the company's registration. The visitor opens the issue or types a question.
2 Explain
In two or three short messages the agent says what it found, and the right side shows the evidence: the return against e-invoices by VAT201 box, the invoice list, a timeline, the supplier status. The visitor can open the invoice list and any invoice as a tax-invoice form, and ask about the case in the "Ask about this case…" field. The step ends with the button "What can I do?".
The assistant shows the FTA letter and explains the cause from the company's own records: ledger against e-invoices, the invoices behind the gap. The visitor can open the invoice list and an invoice form, or the scanned paper invoices, and ask about the case (for example "Why Q3 and not Q4?"). The step ends with "What can I do?".
3 Decide
Under "What you can do" the agent offers two or three options, the recommended one first and highlighted: ask, remind or notify the company, or refer the case to audit. The visitor picks one.
The assistant offers two or three options, the recommended one first: correct with a VD and reply, reply with an explanation only, ask for more time. If the visitor picks a weak option, the assistant says why it will not work and shows the options again.
4 Act
The agent drafts the letter, reminder or notice with the list of invoices attached, and shows the case it will open with its follow-up date. The visitor reads the draft, can edit it, and presses "Send". Nothing goes out without that press.
The assistant prepares the VD box by box (as reported, as corrected, change), the reply to the FTA and, where needed, a message to a supplier or to the ERP team. The visitor checks them and presses "Submit" or "Send".
5 Follow-up
The taxpayer's reply arrives a few days later on its own; the visitor does not wait for it. The agent compares the reply with the data (the VD with the e-invoices, the documents with the amounts requested) and recommends what to do: close, ask for more, or refer to audit. The visitor can open the VD or the documents received.
The FTA's answer arrives a few days later on its own. The assistant says what it means for the company: case closed, amount approved or on hold, anything still open.
6 Closed / Done
The visitor closes the case. The agent shows the case history (who did what and when) and offers the next event on the list or a return to the briefing. After the last event the session summary appears.
The visitor presses "Finish". The summary shows what the visitor did, the result, and the same case as seen on the FTA side, with buttons for the other companies and for the inspector role.

Open question for the development team: can the agent answer free questions on the stand, typed in the visitor's own words? If yes, how, given that every number in an answer must come from the data and not from the language model. If not, the free query is limited to a set of suggested questions.
Scenario 1 needs no free input: the visitor chooses from fixed options, so every invoice and its check results are known in advance and the test data can include them.

3.2 Business: choose a company and company overview

Look and feel: colours in the style of FTA services (dark blue, gold, light grey), tables with grey headers, tiles with a gold top line. It is a style, not a copy of any portal: there are no menus, search bar or other portal elements. The header reads "CDP · FTA Assistant for business".

4. Case 1 · Your own e-invoice, live
Company: Najmat Al Khaleej Retail LLC, a seller of office furniture in Dubai.
The visitor issues an e-invoice on the stand and sees within seconds what the FTA sees and what its checks found. The invoice is not issued in an FTA system. It is issued in the seller's own e-invoicing app, a simple illustrative interface of an e-invoicing provider, and then opens in the FTA system with the check results. Times are the real time of the stand; in the example it is Thursday 10.12.2026, 11:42. The business side comes first here, because the invoice starts there.
The visitor types nothing. The app has three short steps: the buyer, what to sell with quantities set by + and −, and the VAT category and rate for each line. The VAT step is filled in from the seller's item list, and two items in that list carry the wrong code, so the errors come from the data, as they often do in practice. The visitor can change any line. The app offers only valid pairs of category and rate and checks the format, as real invoicing software does, so the stand shows no format or arithmetic errors. The errors it shows are about the content and are found only on the FTA side.

Why this case is realistic. Invoicing software checks the format, not whether a tax code fits the goods, so a wrong code in the item list goes out on every invoice with that item. A wrong rate inside a category, for example S at 0%, is caught mechanically, because category and rate disagree. Category Z at 0% and category E are formally valid, and zero-rated and exempt domestic supplies do exist, such as qualifying healthcare and education or residential rent. So these errors show only in context: furniture delivered in the UAE with no export, installation that is not on the exempt list. That is why the agent says "likely" and the FTA asks the seller to correct the invoice or explain in writing. The TRN flag uses data the FTA already holds: the buyer's VAT registration status.
